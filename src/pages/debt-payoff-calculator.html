    Snowball vs avalanche: which is better?

    Both methods have you pay the minimum on every debt and put all your extra money toward one target debt. When that debt is gone, its payment rolls onto the next one, so your payments grow like a snowball.

    • Avalanche: target the debt with the highest interest rate first. This always costs the least interest overall and is usually the fastest.
    • Snowball: target the smallest balance first. You pay a little more interest, but you clear whole debts sooner, and those quick wins keep many people motivated.

    The calculator runs both so you can see the real difference for your debts. If it's small, pick whichever you're more likely to stick with.

    How it's calculated

    Each month, interest is added to every balance (annual rate ÷ 12), the minimum is paid on each debt, and everything left over, including your extra amount and any minimums freed up by debts you've cleared, goes to the target debt. This repeats until every balance reaches zero.

    Tips for getting out of debt faster

    • Stop adding new debt while you pay off the old.
    • Ask for a lower rate. Lenders sometimes reduce rates for customers with a good repayment record, and refinancing a high-rate debt can save a lot.
    • Put windfalls on the target debt. Bonuses, gifts and side income shorten the timeline quickly.
    • Keep a small emergency fund so a surprise expense doesn't push you back into borrowing.

    Frequently asked questions

    What if my minimum payment doesn't cover the interest?
    Then that debt grows every month, even with payments. The calculator warns you if this happens. Increase the payment on that debt, or ask the lender about a lower rate or payment plan.
    Should I save or pay off debt first?
    Many people keep a small emergency fund first, then attack high-interest debt, because the interest saved on a debt charging 20% or more is usually higher than what savings earn.
    Does this include fees?
    No. It uses the balance, interest rate and payments you enter. If a debt has monthly fees, add them to that debt's minimum payment.

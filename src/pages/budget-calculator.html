What is the 50/30/20 rule?

The 50/30/20 rule is a simple way to budget without tracking every purchase. You split your take-home pay into three groups:

  • 50% for needs: things you must pay to live and work, like rent, basic food, transport, utilities, school fees, insurance and minimum loan payments.
  • 30% for wants: things that make life enjoyable but you could cut, like eating out, entertainment, new clothes, subscriptions and travel.
  • 20% for savings: your emergency fund, savings goals, investments and any extra debt repayment.

Example: with a take-home income of 300,000 a month, you'd aim for 150,000 on needs, 90,000 on wants and 60,000 in savings.

When to use a different split

The 50/30/20 rule is a starting point, not a law. Where rent and food take a big share of income, 60/20/20 or even 70/20/10 may be more realistic. If you're paying off debt or saving for something big, 40/30/30 speeds things up. Choose a rule above and see what fits.

How to make your budget work

  • Pay yourself first. Move your savings amount to a separate account or mobile money wallet on payday, before spending anything.
  • Build an emergency fund first. Aim for three to six months of needs, so surprises don't push you into debt.
  • Review monthly. Compare your real spending with the plan and adjust. Small changes add up.
  • If needs are above 50%, look at the biggest items first, like rent, transport and loans. That's where changes make the most difference.

Frequently asked questions

Should I use income before or after tax?
Use your take-home pay, the amount that actually reaches your bank account or wallet after tax and deductions.
Is paying off debt a need or savings?
Minimum payments are needs. Anything you pay above the minimum counts towards the savings 20%, because it improves your financial position just like saving does.
My income changes every month. How do I budget?
Budget based on your lowest typical month, and save anything extra in good months. A bigger emergency fund is especially important with irregular income.

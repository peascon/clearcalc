The fee is only half the cost

Money transfer services make money in two ways: the fee they show you, and the exchange rate markup they don't. If the real market rate is 1 dollar = 1,400 francs but a provider gives you 1,330, it keeps 70 francs on every dollar, which is 5% of your money, even when it advertises "zero fees".

This calculator adds both together so you can compare providers fairly by the only number that matters: how much your recipient actually receives.

How the total cost is calculated

Recipient gets = (Amount − deducted fee) × Provider rate
Hidden cost = Amount converted × (Market rate − Provider rate) ÷ Market rate
Total cost = Fee + Hidden cost

The market rate (also called the mid-market or interbank rate) is the rate halfway between what banks buy and sell a currency for. It's the rate you see when you search a currency pair on Google or a currency site, and the fairest benchmark.

Tips to send money for less

  • Compare every time. Rates and fees change daily, and the cheapest provider for 50 may not be cheapest for 500.
  • Send larger amounts less often. Fixed fees hurt most on small transfers.
  • Check how the money arrives. Mobile money (like MTN MoMo or M-Pesa), bank deposit and cash pickup can have different rates and fees with the same provider.
  • Watch for receiving fees. Some banks charge the recipient to accept an international transfer.
  • Avoid card-funded transfers if your card issuer treats them as cash advances, which can add fees and interest.

Frequently asked questions

Where do I find each provider's exchange rate?
Start a transfer on the provider's website or app; most show the exact rate and fee before you pay. Enter those numbers here to compare.
What is a good total cost for sending money?
The United Nations' Sustainable Development Goals target an average remittance cost of 3% or less. Many providers charge more than that, especially for small transfers, so it pays to compare.
Why is the cost percentage different from the fee?
Because it includes the exchange rate markup. A provider charging no fee but a 4% weaker rate costs more than one charging a 2% fee at the market rate.
Is my information stored?
No. All calculations happen in your browser and nothing is sent anywhere.

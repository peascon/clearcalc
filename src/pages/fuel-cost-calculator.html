How to calculate fuel cost

You need three numbers: how far you're going, how much fuel your vehicle uses, and the fuel price.

Fuel needed (L) = Distance (km) × L/100km ÷ 100
Fuel cost = Fuel needed × Price per litre

Example: a 120 km trip there and back is 240 km. A car using 8 L/100 km burns 240 × 8 ÷ 100 = 19.2 litres. At 1.50 per litre, the trip costs about 28.80.

Finding your fuel efficiency

  • Check the manual or manufacturer's website for your model's official figure. Real-world use is usually 10–20% higher.
  • Measure it yourself: fill the tank, note the odometer, drive normally, then fill up again. Divide the litres you added by the kilometres driven and multiply by 100 to get L/100 km.
  • Typical figures: small cars often use 5–7 L/100 km, SUVs 8–12, and motorbikes 2–4 (25–50 km per litre).

Ways to spend less on fuel

  • Drive smoothly. Hard acceleration and braking can raise fuel use noticeably.
  • Keep tyres properly inflated and the engine serviced.
  • Remove extra weight and roof racks you're not using.
  • Share rides. Use the "people sharing" box to see each person's fair share.

Frequently asked questions

What's the difference between L/100 km and km/L?
They're two ways of saying the same thing. L/100 km is how many litres you need for 100 km, where lower is better. km/L is how far one litre takes you, where higher is better. 10 km/L equals 10 L/100 km, and 20 km/L equals 5 L/100 km.
Does this include other car costs?
No, only fuel. Insurance, maintenance, tyres, parking and tolls add to the real cost of driving.
Can I use it for a motorbike or boda boda?
Yes. Choose km/L and enter how many kilometres it does per litre, often 30–50 for small motorbikes.

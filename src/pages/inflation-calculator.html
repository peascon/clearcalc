What is inflation?

Inflation is the general rise in prices over time. When prices go up, each unit of money buys less. That's why bread, transport or school fees cost more today than they did ten years ago. Most central banks aim to keep inflation low and stable, often around 2–5% a year, though it can be much higher in some years and countries.

How it's calculated

Inflation compounds, just like interest. Each year's price rise builds on the year before:

Future price = Today's price × (1 + inflation ÷ 100)years
Value today = Past amount × (1 + inflation ÷ 100)years

Example: at 5% inflation, something costing 100 today will cost about 163 in 10 years. Put the other way, 100 kept in cash for 10 years will buy only about 61% of what it buys today.

How to protect your money from inflation

  • Don't keep long-term savings only in cash. Money that earns nothing loses value every year.
  • Earn a rate above inflation. Compare savings accounts, fixed deposits and government bonds. The real return is roughly the interest rate minus inflation.
  • Plan future goals in future prices. If school fees rise every year, save for what they'll cost, not what they cost today.
  • Ask for raises that keep up. A raise below inflation is really a pay cut in buying power.

Frequently asked questions

What inflation rate should I use?
Use a recent multi-year average for your country, which central banks and statistics offices publish. For long-term planning, many people test a few rates to see a range of outcomes.
What is the rule of 70?
Divide 70 by the inflation rate to estimate how many years it takes for prices to double. At 5% inflation, prices double in about 14 years.
Is this exact for my country's history?
No. This calculator uses one average rate for every year. Real inflation changes year to year, so official historical price data will give slightly different results.

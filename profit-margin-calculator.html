Margin vs markup: what's the difference?

Both measure profit, but they divide by different numbers, and mixing them up is one of the most common pricing mistakes.

  • Margin is profit as a share of the selling price. It tells you how much of every sale you keep.
  • Markup is profit as a share of the cost. It tells you how much you added on top of what you paid.
Margin = (Price − Cost) ÷ Price × 100
Markup = (Price − Cost) ÷ Cost × 100

Example: you buy an item for 60 and sell it for 100. Your profit is 40, so your margin is 40% (40 ÷ 100) but your markup is 66.7% (40 ÷ 60). A 40% markup would only give you a 28.6% margin.

How to price for a target margin

To earn a specific margin, divide the cost by (1 − margin):

Price = Cost ÷ (1 − Margin ÷ 100)

For a 40% margin on an item costing 60: 60 ÷ 0.6 = 100. Adding 40% to the cost (60 × 1.4 = 84) would give only a 28.6% margin.

Tips for healthier margins

  • Count all your costs. Include transport, packaging, payment fees and losses, not just the purchase price.
  • Watch VAT. If you're VAT-registered, calculate margin on prices excluding VAT. Use the VAT calculator to remove it first.
  • Buy in bulk carefully. A lower unit cost raises margin, but only if the stock sells.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good profit margin?
It depends heavily on the industry. Grocery and fuel businesses often run on single-digit margins and make money through volume, while services, software and handmade goods often have margins above 50%. Compare yourself with similar businesses.
Can margin be more than 100%?
No. Margin is a share of the selling price, so it can never reach 100% unless the cost is zero. Markup has no upper limit.
Is this gross or net margin?
This calculator shows gross margin per item: price minus the direct cost of the item. Net margin also subtracts overheads like rent, salaries and marketing.
